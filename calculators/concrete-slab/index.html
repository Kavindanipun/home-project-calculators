How this concrete slab calculator works

A rectangular slab is a volume problem. The calculator multiplies length by width by thickness, then converts the result into cubic yards and cubic meters. Because thickness is usually entered in inches in the United States, it is converted to feet before the multiplication.

Concrete slab formula

Cubic feet = length (ft) × width (ft) × thickness (in ÷ 12)
Cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27

The waste allowance is applied after the geometric volume is calculated. That gives you a planning quantity rather than pretending every pour matches the drawing perfectly.

Example: 12 ft × 10 ft × 4 in

A 4-inch thickness is 0.333 ft. Multiplying 12 × 10 × 0.333 gives about 40 ft³. Dividing by 27 gives about 1.48 yd³. With 10% added, the planning quantity is about 1.63 yd³.

Bagged concrete equivalents

For convenience, the result also shows approximate bag counts. Exact yield varies by product, so use the yield printed on the specific bag you intend to buy as the final check.

Common questions

Should I always add 10% waste?

No. Ten percent is a convenient planning default, not a universal rule. Site conditions, excavation accuracy, spillage and the shape of the pour can change the amount of extra material you need.

Can I use this result to order ready-mix?

Use it as a starting estimate, then confirm minimum-load, delivery and ordering requirements with the supplier.
