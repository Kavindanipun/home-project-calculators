How this driveway gravel calculator works

Gravel volume is length × width × depth. Since driveway depth is usually entered in inches, the tool converts it to feet. Cubic feet are divided by 27 to get cubic yards. An allowance is then added for compaction, uneven grade and handling.

Gravel formulas

Cubic yards = length × width × (depth ÷ 12) ÷ 27
Tons = cubic yards × material density

The density field is intentionally editable because crushed stone, pea gravel and other aggregates do not all weigh the same.

Why depth matters

On a 50 × 12 ft driveway, increasing depth by one inch adds 50 cubic feet before any allowance. Across a large area, small depth changes can substantially change both cubic yards and tons.

Common questions

What density should I use?

Use the density or tons-per-cubic-yard value supplied for the exact material you plan to order. The default is only a generic starting point.

Does this include compaction?

The allowance field can be used as a planning buffer, but actual compaction depends on the material, moisture and installation method.
