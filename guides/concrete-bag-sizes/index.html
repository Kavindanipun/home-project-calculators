40, 60 and 80 lb Concrete Bag Sizes Explained

Compare common concrete bag sizes, approximate yields and what changes the number of bags you need for a project.

Published September 20, 2026 · BuildEstimate Editorial Team
How this guide was prepared: We use standard geometry and clearly stated planning assumptions. Where product yield matters, verify the exact manufacturer label before purchase. BuildEstimate is not an engineering or building-code authority.

Why bag size changes the count

Concrete bag calculators are simple in principle: project volume is divided by the amount of mixed concrete one bag produces. The important detail is that a 40 lb bag, 60 lb bag and 80 lb bag do not produce the same volume. That is why the same slab can require very different bag counts.

Common approximate yields

Bag sizeApprox. yield
40 lb0.30 ft³
60 lb0.45 ft³
80 lb0.60 ft³

These values are published for QUIKRETE Concrete Mix and are useful for planning, but formulations vary. Always read the yield stated on the actual product bag you are buying.

Source: QUIKRETE Concrete Mix SPEC-DATA.

How to compare bag sizes

Start with required cubic feet including your chosen allowance. Divide by the stated yield per bag, then round up. To compare cost, multiply the rounded bag count by the current price per bag—not the theoretical unrounded count.

Bags needed = required ft³ ÷ yield per bag, rounded up

Handling vs fewer bags

Smaller bags may be easier to move and mix, but you will handle more of them. Larger bags reduce bag count but increase individual lifting weight. The best choice depends on the project, equipment and people doing the work.

Do not ignore water and mixing instructions

Yield assumes the product is mixed according to its instructions. Adding excessive water or changing the mix is not a valid way to stretch the stated yield.
